Local energy is where decarbonization gets built. Nobody should build it alone.

A proposed member-owned alliance of energy community networks, cities, research centres, grid operators and companies, giving local energy actors three things they cannot easily get alone: a shared commons, a project pipeline and a common voice.

01 · Why now

Policy, money and technology are lining up on the local level.

Three shifts make local energy the place where the transition is won or lost, and make the next few years a rare window to organise.

9 → 90 GW
Policy. The European Commission's 2026 Citizens Energy Package aims for roughly a tenfold rise in community-owned renewables by 2030. It is voluntary, so delivery depends on capacity on the ground.
8,000+
Scale. Energy communities counted in the EU by the Commission (an academic mapping counts about 3,900: definitions differ). Cities, regions and grid operators are moving too.
Twins
Technology and funding. EU programmes already fund digital twins on the grid side (e.g. TwinEU, DSO4DT) and community facilities separately. The bridge between them is missing.

Sources: European Commission, REScoop.eu, mapping study (Sustainability 2023), project sites. See references.

02 · The problem

The obstacle is not effort. It is duplication and isolation.

Reviews keep finding the same pattern: tools and methods exist but are fragmented, hard to connect and rarely built for communities; start-ups lack expertise; evidence stays inside each project; and no one speaks for local energy systems with one voice.

03 · The proposal

An alliance with three jobs.

Digital twins are the shared language and the first thing we build, but the Alliance is about what organisations can do together. Select a job.

1

Commons

Shared, open building blocks that no one wins by keeping private.

2

Pipeline

A way to turn ideas into funded projects, with the right partners.

3

Voice

One credible, evidence-based voice towards EU, IEA, OECD and city networks.

The commons starts where the literature is strongest: shared data models, flexibility and grid-interaction methods, common KPIs and validation. These are examples, not the whole proposal.

04 · Why an alliance

Compared with the alternatives, a member-owned alliance fills the gap.

We are not replacing anything. The IEA Annex is the first engine, the Alliance is its permanent, wider home. Highlight a requirement you care about.

Assessment is our reading and open to challenge. IEA programmes are agreements between governments and have no legal personality of their own.

05 · Who takes part, and what they get

Five kinds of members, each with a clear reason to join.

Joining early

Early members fund the work before it is proven, so they would get a founders' package. The shape is still open:

Rate

A reduced fee locked for several years.

Seat

A reserved role in the first board and technical committee.

Credit

Part of the fee returned as services such as training or proposal support.

Fee levels are undecided and left blank on purpose. Umbrella networks would pay little or nothing in cash; their contribution is membership reach, validation and legitimacy.

06 · How it is run

Neutral by design, so competitors can sit at the same table.

A short charter

  • Open by default for the shared core
  • Members keep their proprietary work
  • No services that compete with members
  • Competition-law guidance for all meetings

Balanced bodies

  • Assembly of all members
  • Board balanced across the five member families
  • Technical committee for the commons
  • Small secretariat

Fair play on projects

  • Lead roles rotate with clear criteria
  • The Alliance facilitates, never picks winners
  • A share of won projects returns to the commons (if funders allow)

Legal form starts as a Spanish association (fast and low cost) and could move to an international non-profit when membership and funding justify it.

07 · Long term

From first proof to a reference body.

2027–2029 · Prove

Show it works

2030–2032 · Scale

Become the default

Release cycle, regional hubs, framework agreements with cities and grid operators.

2033–2036 · Institutionalise

Stable reference

Standards cited by funders and regulators, stable multi-year funding.
